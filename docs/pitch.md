# Elevator Pitch

## Final (<200 characters)

> HardEars fine-tunes NVIDIA Nemotron to better transcribe Bajan speech, local accents, vocabulary and place names—making Barbadian audio more searchable, accessible and AI-ready.

168 characters.

## Long version (under 200 words)

> We're building HardEars, a speech recognition model fine-tuned specifically for Bajan English and Barbadian audio.
>
> Today's leading ASR systems perform well on standard English, but often struggle with Caribbean accents, local vocabulary, place names, code-switching and fast conversational speech. That matters in Barbados, where valuable information lives in radio, interviews, community media and everyday spoken conversation that is effectively invisible to search and AI systems.
>
> Starting from NVIDIA's Nemotron speech model, we're creating a high-quality Barbados-focused training set, combining real local audio with carefully corrected transcripts. We then fine-tune and evaluate the model against the base system to measure improvements in word accuracy, local entity recognition and dialect understanding.
>
> The goal is not just a better transcription model. HardEars is infrastructure for making Bajan speech machine-readable — unlocking better search, media monitoring, cultural preservation, accessibility and locally grounded AI.
>
> If the internet is increasingly becoming multimodal, Barbados shouldn't have to change how it speaks to be understood.

## Iteration note

The long version was cut down to a sub-200-character version for places that constrain length (e.g. social bios, repository descriptions, hackathon one-liners). The cut preserves the four load-bearing claims:

1. What it is (fine-tune of Nemotron)
2. For whom (Bajan speech, accents, vocabulary, place names)
3. Why it matters (Barbadian audio becomes searchable, accessible, AI-ready)
4. (Implicit) the underlying thesis about Barbadians not having to change how they speak.
